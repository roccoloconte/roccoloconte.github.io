Positioning in Modern Networks

August 14, 2025

Network-based positioning has become essential as we move beyond GPS-dependent systems. From tracking assets in warehouses to monitoring livestock in remote farms, modern applications need positioning that works indoors, consumes minimal power, and costs little per device. This shift has driven innovations across modern networks like cellular, Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, Ultra-Wideband, and IoT networks.

GPS Positioning

GPS relies on a constellation of satellites orbiting Earth. The system works through a process called multilateration, where the device measures the time it takes for signals to travel from at least four satellites. Since radio waves travel at the speed of light, these time measurements can be converted directly into distances.

Each distance measurement creates an imaginary sphere around that satellite, and the device's position is located where these spheres intersect. The fourth satellite serves a critical purpose beyond positioning — it corrects for clock errors in the device. While satellites carry atomic clocks accurate to nanoseconds, the phone or GPS receiver uses much less precise timing.

The satellites continuously broadcast their precise orbital positions along with timestamps. The device performs the calculation and once distances to multiple satellites are known, basic geometry determines the location through the intersection of these spherical measurements.

Why Network-Based Positioning Matters

The GPS Problem

GPS works well for smartphones and cars, but fails in modern IoT scenarios:

  • Power consumption: GPS chips drain batteries in weeks, not years
  • Indoor performance: Signals can't penetrate buildings effectively
  • Cost: Adding GPS increases device cost

The New Requirements

Modern IoT applications need:

  • Indoor/outdoor operation without signal gaps
  • Battery life measured in years, not months
  • Low-cost devices for mass deployment
  • Reasonable accuracy for application needs (not centimeter precision)

5G Positioning

Multiple measurement types working together:

  • Time-based: Measuring how long signals take to travel between device and multiple base stations
  • Angle-based: Using massive antenna arrays to determine signal direction
  • Hybrid approaches: Combining multiple techniques for better accuracy

5G networks deploy many more base stations than previous generations. More base stations mean better geometry for triangulation — similar to how GPS needs multiple satellites for accurate positioning.

This type of positioning requires dense network deployment and high-end devices. This makes it suitable for smartphones and vehicles, but not for low-cost IoT sensors.

Wi-Fi Positioning

Wi-Fi positioning has become crucial for indoor positioning, especially in smartphones and enterprise applications.

It uses received signal strength (RSSI) measurements from multiple known access points:

  • Device scans for nearby Wi-Fi networks
  • Measures received signal strength from each access point
  • Uses RSSI-based ranging to estimate distances and calculate position
  • Can also use fingerprinting techniques that map signal patterns to locations

Wi-Fi positioning works best in environments with dense access point coverage:

  • Shopping malls and airports for navigation
  • Warehouses for asset tracking
  • Office buildings for device management
  • Hospitals for equipment and patient monitoring

The main advantage is leveraging existing Wi-Fi infrastructure without additional hardware. However, accuracy depends heavily on access point density and environmental factors affecting signal propagation.

Bluetooth Positioning

Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE) positioning excels at micro-positioning scenarios where precise indoor positioning is needed.

Most BLE positioning uses strategically placed beacons:

  • Small battery-powered devices transmit signals at regular intervals
  • Mobile devices measure signal strength from multiple beacons
  • Position calculated using signal strength-based ranging or proximity detection
  • Battery life of beacons can exceed several years

BLE positioning targets specific applications:

  • Retail analytics and customer behavior tracking
  • Asset tracking within rooms or specific areas
  • Museum and exhibition guidance systems
  • Industrial equipment monitoring in confined spaces

The technology provides room-level or sub-room accuracy, making it ideal for applications where it's necessary to know not just which building, but which specific area within that building.

Ultra-Wideband (UWB) Positioning

Ultra-Wideband represents the gold standard for high-precision indoor positioning, offering centimeter-level accuracy with low latency.

UWB's unique characteristics enable superior positioning:

  • Uses very short pulses across a wide frequency spectrum
  • Precise time-of-flight measurements between devices
  • Resistant to multipath interference that degrades other technologies
  • Can achieve sub-10cm accuracy in optimal conditions

UWB positioning applications:

  • Industrial automation requiring precise equipment positioning
  • Automotive keyless entry and safety systems
  • Consumer electronics (integrated into recent iPhones and Android devices)
  • Healthcare for patient and equipment tracking
  • Augmented reality applications requiring precise spatial awareness

The main limitations are higher power consumption than BLE and shorter range, but UWB excels where precision matters more than battery life or coverage area.

LPWAN Positioning

LoRaWAN Positioning

Time Difference of Arrival (TDoA): This is how most LoRaWAN positioning works:

  • Device transmits a single packet
  • Multiple gateways receive the packet at slightly different times
  • Time differences reveal the device's position relative to gateways

The main hurdle is synchronization between gateways — all gateways need extremely accurate clocks. Even tiny timing errors translate to large position errors since radio waves travel 300 meters per microsecond.

LoRaWAN TDoA real-world performance varies widely based on:

  • Number and placement of gateways
  • Environmental obstacles
  • Clock synchronization quality

When TDoA isn't available, systems fall back to Received Signal Strength Indicator (RSSI) measurements:

  • RSSI estimates distance based on signal strength - stronger signals generally mean shorter distances
  • Less accurate than TDoA but works with fewer gateways
  • Provides a fallback positioning method when precise timing synchronization isn't achievabe

NB-IoT Positioning

NB-IoT has a fundamental limitation for positioning: devices typically communicate with only a single cell tower at a time. Therefore, positioning must be derived from signals of that one tower, unlike LoRaWAN which can use multiple gateways simultaneously.

How NB-IoT positioning works:

  • Device connects to the nearest cell tower
  • System estimates distance based on signal strength and timing
  • Cell tower's directional antenna provides rough direction
  • Position estimated as somewhere within that sector

However, radio signals behave unpredictably in real environments:

  • Buildings cause reflections and shadows
  • Weather affects signal propagation
  • Interference from other devices creates noise

These environmental factors make NB-IoT positioning challenging, typically providing city-block level accuracy rather than precise positioning.

Comparisons

GPS Outdoor tracking, navigation High accuracy, global coverage Indoor limitations, power consumption

Conclusion

The landscape of positioning technologies demonstrates that there is no universal solution. The optimal choice depends entirely on the application's specific requirements for accuracy, power consumption, cost, and operating environment.

GPS remains dominant for outdoor navigation, while 5G promises high-precision tracking for premium applications. Wi-Fi and BLE leverage existing infrastructure for cost-effective indoor positioning, and UWB delivers unmatched precision where accuracy justifies the cost. For IoT deployments requiring years of battery life, LPWAN technologies like LoRaWAN and NB-IoT provide essential positioning capabilities despite their accuracy trade-offs.

As machine learning techniques mature, we can expect significant improvements in positioning accuracy across all these technologies, particularly for challenging indoor and urban environments where signal propagation is complex and unpredictable.
